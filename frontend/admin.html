Areas

Together: the climbs on one rock are harvested, then meshed from all their photos as one mesh, which shows each from where its own photos were taken; a climb it hardly shows is meshed on its own too. Where the page names several rocks (a sector, "… Area", "… Boulders") or doesn't say, each climb is meshed on its own, and once they're through, those whose photos show the same rock are meshed together as well. A climb alone on its rock is meshed on its own either way.
Most of a harvest's time on a small server. Pairs matched before are kept, for each way, so a climb harvested again only matches what's new. On 12 of hal's harvests FLANN kept 92 of the 101 search photos brute force matched (and one it didn't), and more checks didn't close the gap, so brute force stays the default.
For meshes made from now on, here and on machines that connect here. Every other step is the same either way (GPU SIFT, OpenMVS 2.4 with one geometric pass, thinned texturing). The global mapper can place a loosely linked group of photos (one video, say) in the wrong spot, or two halves of a boulder a few degrees apart, which makes ghost sheets; its cameras that disagree with the rest are dropped and a split placing is redone incrementally, but that doesn't catch every case (Caveman Boulder). The incremental one takes 10-25 minutes longer on a big boulder.
A boulder's mesh tries the photos it leaves out again against itself. Of what's still left, each set COLMAP places together (6 photos or more) is a scene to mesh apart, as another side or rock, if it's tied to the boulder: it holds a climb's Mountain Project page photo, or 2 of its photos match photos the mesh placed (16 verified matches, as the off-topic filter takes one). Its card says boulder only when no such scene waits or made a mesh (one tried that made nothing doesn't count), and every climb on it was harvested and tried in it. This queues each never tried, or cut short. Meshes from before those were recorded go by the climbs with 6 or more photos left out, and stay groups until meshed again (the button above, for all of them, or a climb's card's Mesh the boulder again), which checks what they left out; nothing meshes those left-out photos as they are, as for Drifter that made a mesh of another rock. Meshing again uses the photos as harvested (only problems on a boulder's page never harvested are), and first reads what each one's Mountain Project area is, where that never was: one whose page doesn't say it's one rock isn't meshed whole again, as its climbs are meshed on their own and pooled by their photos.

Compute

Each worker takes one area's climb at a time, and harvests it here, off-topic filter and all. this server then meshes it here (with reconstructions above 0), and also runs the jobs asked for on this page; each remote hands its climbs to a machine, one at a time: Climbs at once meshed on other machines is how many of those workers there are, so how many climbs can be on their way at once (one harvested here while another is meshed), even with one machine. One idle has no climb it can take now: the rest are waiting for volunteers' videos, for others on their boulder, or for each other. Cores for this server's own processing keeps harvests, the filter, packing meshes and finding beta here to that many of its cores (the last ones), leaving the first for visitors: all by default. Whatever it is, this server filters one harvest's photos at a time, and the public setup holds its native libraries to one thread each (OMP_NUM_THREADS and the like in docker-compose.public.yml), as two filters at once spun in the kernel for 5 hours on Nov 12, 2026.

EC2 machines that connect here

Each runs the worker script on an EC2 GPU instance with a token of its own, so it works like the machines above, alongside them. It runs until you end it here: nothing starts or ends one by itself, though AWS can take back a spot instance (you're told). It uses the AWS settings above (region, spot, disk, AMI, subnet, security group) and the AWS credentials on this page, and needs this server's address for machines (WORKER_URL, else VOLUNTEER_URL or SITE_HOST). Its first climb waits for the image to download (about 18 GB).
What they cost comes out of a balance you top up here: estimated each minute from each one's price (on-demand from AWS's price list, spot from its zone's price, looked up hourly), its disk and its public address, but not data sent out (well under a cent a climb). One starts only with an hour of it left. You're told on your phone with a fifth of the last top-up left. With none left, they're given no new climb and end once theirs is done, or at once $2 past it; a top-up before then lets them carry on.

Volunteers fetching YouTube videos

Volunteers and machines that register themselves

The volunteer and worker scripts make their own tokens and register them here, with a name if their user gave one. Each waits for your approval, in the lists above or from a notification on your phone.
Approved volunteers: at most an hour and a day (the script's own limits are 15 and 200, its videos spread over each hour; searches and channels' pages count apart, 30 an hour and 200 a day)
Check videos against YouTube's storyboards (its small seek-bar frames), fetched by a trusted volunteer: % of those from volunteers not trusted, % of trusted ones'.
A boulder or climb whose videos volunteers are still fetching waits for them; tell the phone once one has waited h (0: never), and again at 3, 6 and 12 times that, then every 12 times more. Nothing is given up on by time: only a video failing for good, or cancelled here, lets it be meshed without. Videos are fetched a boulder at a time, the one waiting longest first: regular areas', then low-priority areas', then beta's (beta waiting 2 days goes before low-priority areas').
Videos that come after a boulder's or pool's mesh was made: it's made again with them once nothing more is coming for its climbs, so that late videos remake it once, not once each.
Volunteers run yt-dlp's builds (nightly follows YouTube's changes days or weeks before a release; stable to roll back. Scripts switch at their next ask for work)

Where the site is opened from

Each time the main page opens in a browser (its script, so not most crawlers), the server keeps roughly where from, as Cloudflare places the address (city, to about 10 km), and the site that linked there: not the address or the browser. Editors' own (the token's) aren't counted. Kept in data/page-views.jsonl.

Comments and bug reports

People

Accounts, each made from an invite: whoever opens its link picks a name, accepts the terms, and may then make their own sets of beta. A moderator's areas are the ids of the areas they may approve additions to, or * for all. An editor (role owner) may change anything the editing token can, with no storage limit, and what they add is approved as it comes. An uploader is a user whose uploads are approved as they come (as a moderator's are). Ticking Mesh next for an account meshes what it adds as the main site's Mesh next would: ahead of everything else, once it's approved.
An account may hold at most MB of what it added (approved things don't count; an account's own quota, in the table, goes before this)

Uploads' areas, from their GPS

Uploads not of a climb in an area are put in the area their photos' GPS is in: one listed here, or else Mountain Project's area of the place. These were put there automatically and haven't been looked over: until they are, that area's moderators can't approve them. Where several areas are close by, the one listing more boulder problems than routes was chosen, and its uploader is asked which.

Footage to ask for

Climbers whose ascents climbing-history.org's pages give only Instagram posts for, which can't be fetched. Each has a message drafted to copy, change and send yourself: nothing is sent from here. One message a climber, covering all their climbs. Mark what you've sent, so nobody is asked twice.
